What the ANVUR tables contain

The Padova institutional report includes the following tables, which form the basis of this analysis:

  • Table 2.1 (R1): Quality indicator for tenured staff, by department and disciplinary area (area CUN).
  • Table 2.2 (R2): Quality indicator for newly recruited / promoted staff.
  • Table 2.3 (R1_2): Quality indicator for all evaluated staff combined.
  • Table 2.4: Summary indicators (H, R, IRD) aggregated across disciplinary areas for all 32 departments.

Data Extraction

Data were extracted in two ways:

  1. Area-level indicators (R1, R2, R1_2 by department within Areas 05 and 06): Manually transcribed from the PDF report into vqr_area_data.csv. The pipeline script also includes a PDF parsing function (parse_area_tables()) using pdftools and regex, though the verified CSV is used by default.

  2. Department-level indicators (all 32 departments): Transcribed into vqr_ird_data.csv from Table 2.4 of the institutional report.

WarningData extraction caveats
  • R1 data for Area 05 includes only 5 of the 7 departments. BCA and DNS are excluded because they had fewer than 10 expected products from tenured staff — an ANVUR reporting threshold, not a data gap.
  • All R1 values were added from pages 11–12 of the ANVUR PDF during the analysis. The values should be cross-checked against the original source.

Indicator Definitions

Quality indicator R

The R indicator for a department in a given area is:

\[ R = \frac{\bar{v}_{\text{dept}}}{\bar{v}_{\text{national}}} \]

where \[\bar{v}\] is the average evaluation score of submitted products. R > 1 means the department’s average quality exceeds the national average for that area.

Size-weight indicator H

\[ H = \frac{n_{\text{dept}}}{N_{\text{institution}}} \]

where \[n_{\text{dept}}\] is the number of expected products from the department and \[N_{\text{institution}}\] is the total across the institution. H reflects the department’s relative contribution to the institution’s evaluated output.

Composite indicator IRD

\[ IRD = H \times R \]

This product means that IRD scales with both quality (R) and size (H). A small department with high R can still have a low IRD, because H is small. This is a deliberate design choice by ANVUR: IRD is intended to capture total research impact, not quality per capita.

ImportantCritical note on IRD interpretation

IRD is sometimes used in internal benchmarking as though it measures departmental “performance.” This is misleading. A department with R = 1.26 (top quality) but only 157 expected products will always score lower on IRD than a department with R = 1.05 but 350 products. IRD reflects departmental scale as much as — or more than — research quality. This is explored in detail in the Results.
